The determinant as area and volume experimental

The columns of A span a parallelogram (2×2) or a box (3×3). Its size is |det A| and its orientation is the sign. Change the matrix and watch both.

The matrix A

Drag the blue and vermilion tips. Blue parallelogram: positive determinant. Purple: negative. Grey: zero.

Column operations

Try this
  1. 2×2: drag the blue tip to (2, 0) and the vermilion tip to (0, 2). By what factor did the area grow, and what is det A?
  2. Swap the two columns (blue to the vermilion position and back). What happens to the colour and to the sign?
  3. Press R2 ← R2 + k·R1 with k = 2. The picture is sheared. What happens to the area and to det? Why is the determinant unchanged by this operation?
  4. Press R1 ← k·R1 with k = 3. By what factor does det change?
  5. Make the two columns point along the same line. What is the area? Can you undo it with a row operation?
  6. 3×3: choose Flat (det 0). Why does the box collapse? Which relation do the three columns satisfy?
  7. Press Swap columns 1 and 2 and watch the sign. Is the volume the same?

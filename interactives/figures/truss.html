Forces in a triangular truss

The load P pushes down on joint 2. Drag the top joint (or use the sliders) and watch the three bar forces, which come from solving E f = p.

Blue, T = tension (bar is pulled, f > 0). Vermilion, C = compression (bar is pushed, f < 0). Thickness shows the size of the force.

Try this
  1. Choose the Text example (30°, 60°, P = 10 kN). Compare f1, f2, f3 with your own solution of the exercise.
  2. Choose Symmetric 45°, then lower both angles with the sliders. What happens to the force in the upper bars? Watch det E.
  3. Double P. Which numbers double? Why is that to be expected from a linear system E f = p?
  4. Drag the top joint far to the left, so that θ1 is large and θ2 small. Which upper bar now carries the larger force? Does the closed form f1 = −P cos θ2 / sin(θ1+θ2) agree?
  5. Why is the bottom bar always in tension, whatever the angles?

Systems of linear equations: lines and planes experimental

Each equation is a line (two unknowns) or a plane (three unknowns). A solution is a point that lies on all of them. Change the numbers and see when there is one solution, none, or infinitely many.

Row picture: each equation is a line

Column picture: x·(column 1) + y·(column 2) = b

Drag the picture to turn it (or click it and use the arrow keys). The planes are cut off by a box.

Try this
  1. Choose Parallel lines. Slide the right-hand side of one equation until the lines lie on top of each other. How many solutions are there now?
  2. Choose One solution and tick the third equation. Slide its right-hand side until all three lines meet in one point. How rare is that?
  3. In the column picture, find numbers x and y so that the two scaled columns add up to b. Compare with the intersection point.
  4. Choose Nearly parallel. Change one entry by 0.02. How far does the solution move?
  5. 3×3: choose the three presets with rank 2. In one the planes meet along a line, in the other they form a triangular prism with no common point. What differs in the numbers, and in rank [A | b]?

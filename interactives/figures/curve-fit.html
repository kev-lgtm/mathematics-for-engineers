Curve through points: the Vandermonde system experimental

Drag the points. The polynomial of degree n−1 through n points is found by solving a square linear system whose matrix is built from the x-values. Its determinant tells you when this works.

Try this
  1. Choose Parabola and drag one point. The curve is always a parabola, and the three coefficients change.
  2. Choose Six points on a line. The polynomial has degree at most 5, yet it is a straight line. Which coefficients are zero? Move one point off the line.
  3. Choose Two x-values close. Watch det V, the size of the coefficients and the wild swing of the curve. Then drag the two points to the same x.
  4. With four points, move the first point to the right across the second one. When does det V change sign, and what does that say about the order of the rows?
  5. Why does a matrix whose determinant is nearly 0 give huge coefficients? Think of the formula x = A−1y and of the 1/det in A−1.

Masses and springs: K x = F

Push on the masses with the forces F and watch them settle at the displacements x that make every net force zero. The stiffness matrix K is built from the spring constants.

Displacements are drawn magnified (1 m is shown as one spacing between masses) and are cut off at 0.32 of the spacing; the numbers are always exact.

Try this
  1. Press Text example. Check x1 = 0.1 m and x2 = 0.2 m with the first row of K x = F.
  2. Point at the matrix: blue entries lie on the diagonal, vermilion ones are −kj. Which entries are zero when you switch to three masses, and why?
  3. Make the middle spring very stiff (k2 = 300) and the outer ones soft. Push only mass 1. Does mass 2 follow?
  4. Reverse the sign of F2. What happens to x1 and x2? Which property of the linear system explains this?
  5. The matrix is symmetric. Push mass 1 with 30 N and measure x2; then push mass 2 with 30 N and measure x1. Compare.
